Greensboro Crawl Space Duct Sealing & Airflow Balancing

A vast percentage of homes in Greensboro and Guilford County are built over ventilated crawl spaces where flexible and sheet metal ductwork runs beneath the floorboards. Ripped duct insulation, disconnected boot connections, and unsealed seams draw damp, musty crawl space air and radon directly into home living spaces while dumping conditioned air under the house. Professional duct sealing restores airflow balance and slashes utility costs.

Sealing Duct Air Leaks with Mastic Paste & Fiber Mesh

Standard cloth duct tape dries out, cracks, and fails within three to five years under crawl space humidity. We seal all duct joints, plenum collars, and boot transitions using water-based fiber-reinforced mastic sealant approved to UL 181 standards. This creates a permanent, flexible seal that withstands air pressure changes and crawl space moisture indefinitely.

Airflow Balancing & Room-by-Room Comfort Optimization

Uneven temperatures—such as a freezing master bedroom and a sweltering upstairs nursery—are almost always caused by improper duct static pressure and unbalanced supply runs. Our technicians use digital vane anemometers and flow hoods to measure CFM output at every register, adjusting manual balancing dampers to achieve design airflow.

  • Measurement of individual supply register CFM using calibrated air balancing hoods
  • Installation and adjustment of manual balancing dampers in primary duct branch takeoffs
  • Enlargement of undersized return air plenums to prevent compressor starvation
  • Reduction of high static resistance, extending blower motor and compressor operating life

Frequently asked questions

How much energy can be lost through leaky crawl space ductwork?

According to the Department of Energy, typical residential duct systems lose 20% to 30% of conditioned air through leaks, holes, and poorly connected joints. In unconditioned crawl spaces, this directly inflates monthly electric and gas bills.

What are the telltale signs that my Greensboro home's ductwork is leaking?

Common indicators include rooms that never get comfortable, whistling or booming noises when the blower starts, sudden spikes in energy bills, and dust quickly returning to furniture after cleaning.
